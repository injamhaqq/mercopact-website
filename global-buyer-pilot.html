The commercial problem

Where international buyer development gets stuck.

01

Unclear target-account priorities

Your export team may know the market, but not which companies fit your product, who does the buying there or what order sizes and standards they expect.

02

Research takes time away from selling

Checking company fit, buyer roles and sources can distract technical sales staff from quoting and solving commercial requirements. No database proves intent.

03

Promising conversations lose momentum

Technical questions and quotation requests stall when nobody owns the reply, decides whether it is serious or sets the next follow-up date.

Two starting routes, scoped separately: follow up on enquiries you have already received, where further contact is permitted; or research suitable new accounts in one selected overseas market. Neither an old enquiry nor a company-list entry proves current buyer intent.

Commercial terms

How the pilot is scoped

International projects begin with an individual written scope based on the product, target market, research depth, approved outreach and reporting requirements.

What is agreed in writing

The 30-day period, what you receive, what counts as done, who approves outreach, what you provide and what is excluded are agreed in writing before work begins.

Separate expenses, if required

External data, sending tools, travel, samples or legal advice must be disclosed and approved in advance if required; no optional third-party costs without permission.

30 days. You see the first researched accounts within the first week.
