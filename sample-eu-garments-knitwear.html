Bangladesh Garments and Knitwear: EU Brands That Already Buy From Bangladesh.

A research example for a Bangladesh knit or woven garment factory looking for brand and retail buyers in Germany, Sweden and Belgium.

Independent research example, not a client or buyer. The companies below were identified from their own public reports, websites and published research as research targets only. None has been contacted, none is a MERCOPACT client, and none has said it wants a new supplier. No outreach, RFQ or sales result is claimed.

Supplier category

Bangladesh knitwear and garment factory

Target markets

Germany, Sweden, Belgium

Account status

Research targets only

1. The market hypothesis

Each company below says, in its own report, website or a published interview, that Bangladesh is one of its main production countries. Lindex says a little over 40% of its global volume comes from Bangladesh, Engelbert Strauss reports 41% of its workwear made there, and Stanley/Stella reports 99% of its production value. That shows these companies already buy from Bangladesh. It does not show that any of them needs a new factory.

Sources opened on 22 October 2026. All five already buy from Bangladesh, and four say they have their own team or office there. That is the opening and the obstacle: they already have long-term suppliers, so a new factory needs a clear reason to be added, such as a product gap, spare capacity, certification or price. The first conversation may well happen in Dhaka, not in Europe.

3. Qualification questions

Product-specific fit

Knit or woven, fabric weights, categories (kidswear, basics, workwear, denim) and order sizes each buyer works with, and whether the factory matches them.

Compliance and audits

Which standards each buyer expects (Fair Wear, International Accord or RSC, GOTS, amfori BSCI, Sedex), and which the factory already holds.

Actual buyer authority

Who handles sourcing for each company, in Europe or in its Bangladesh office? No names are presumed here.

Reason to add a supplier

A missing product, capacity, lead time, certification or price. Only a real answer from the buyer turns an account into an opportunity.
